B2B Home IoT Opportunities

From research to business — SmartThings into B2B

Found B2B opportunities through snowball research in Korea and the US, then turned them into a business with smart-apartment UX — 130,000+ households on SmartThings.

Period
2018.09 – 2021.10
Role
B2B IoT research lead; smart-apartment IoT UX (company-wide project, for the Mobile division)
Awards
2020 iF Design Award · 2019 Good Design Award, Silver

Context · Problem

To take B2C-focused SmartThings into B2B, we first had to find where the business opportunity was.

An undefined market

We started without knowing who to sell to, or with what value.

A complex ecosystem

Many stakeholders — developers, constructors, architects, IoT integrators, installers, managers, home-network suppliers, telecoms and residents.

Two countries

Korea and the US differ in housing and decision structures, so the answers don't carry over.

Role · Approach

Snowball research that grows from a hypothesis

Role: B2B IoT research lead; smart-apartment IoT UX (company-wide project, for the Mobile division)

  1. 01

    Hypothesis and pilot

    Started from a hypothesis → first internal workshop (2018.11) → pilot IDIs (US 9, Korea 6).

  2. 02

    Redefining the domains

    At the US workshop (2018.12), the Korea creative workshop (2019.01) and a second internal workshop, redefined the research domains as stakeholders, devices and context, and business logic.

  3. 03

    A large main study

    Ran IDIs, shadowing and home visits at a scale of US 25+16 (San Francisco, Atlanta, Dallas, LA) and Korea 24+16.

  4. 04

    Synthesis workshops

    Wrapped up conclusions and opportunity areas at synthesis workshops in the US (2019.02) and Korea (2019.03).

Key Design · US stakeholders

US — developers decide

  1. 1

    Large multifamily developers

    In the US, large multifamily developers were the key target who decide on smart-home adoption.

  2. 2

    Everyone else advises

    The owner (investor), design team, contractors and property management propose or advise to the developer.

  3. 3

    Cost, energy and ROI

    Winning developers over hinged on proving cost and energy savings and ROI.

  4. 4

    Opportunity — US MFU

    Multifamily units (MFU) became the US B2B opportunity area.

Key Design · Korea stakeholders

Korea — constructors decide

  1. 1

    Constructors decide

    In Korea, the constructor was the key decision maker for adopting Home IoT.

  2. 2

    Before sale and after move-in

    Before sale, developers, architects and home-network suppliers work with the constructor; after move-in, the residents' committee and management company take over operations.

  3. 3

    Air quality matters most

    For Korean residents, air quality was the strongest value.

  4. 4

    Opportunity — Korean smart apartments

    Smart apartments became the Korean B2B opportunity area and went on to become a business.
