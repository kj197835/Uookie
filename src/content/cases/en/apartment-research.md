background:
  problem: We had to verify how SmartThings, built into smart apartments, is really used in daily life — and find and fix the causes of low adoption.
  context:
    - title: Three complexes
      body: Studied residents of Raemian Leaders One, Songdo The Sharp Triple Tower and Hyochang Park Prugio with the same procedure.
    - title: Low adoption
      body: Even with SmartThings built in, few households actually connected and used it.
    - title: Real use
      body: We needed to see real moments of use at home that a survey alone can't reveal.
approach:
  heading: As UX researcher — design, operation, analysis and fixes, hands-on
  items:
    - title: Research design
      body: Set the study goals and participants, and designed every research tool — recruitment notice, screener and quotas, study guide, survey, focus-group guide and home-visit guide.
    - title: Pilot → second study
      body: Validated the method with a first pilot, then improved it and added usability researchers for a second usability study.
    - title: Running three sites
      body: With research agency Ipsos Korea, ran the same procedure at three complexes, from recruitment to home visits.
    - title: Analysis and fixes
      body: Analyzed quantitative and qualitative results into insights, derived improvements and applied them to the product.
design:
  - label: Research design
    layout: wide
    heading: 1st pilot → 2nd usability study — six-step mixed methods
    visual:
      id: apt-research-process
      diagram: apt-research-process
      description: The six-step procedure of the second usability study, improved after the pilot (same at all three complexes)
    points:
      - title: Designed from recruitment
        body: Recruited via a management-office-approved notice, screener and quotas.
      - title: Let them really use it
        body: Five tasks over three days after sign-up — control, alerts, modes, automation, family invites.
      - title: Survey → FGD → visits
        body: Selected from the survey (~30 homes per site) into focus groups (2 × 4 homes), then home visits.
      - title: A repeatable process
        body: Established it as a repeatable research process that works the same way at all three complexes.
  - label: Insight → improvement
    heading: Turning the hurdles we found into improvements
    visual:
      id: apt-insight-map
      diagram: apt-insight-map
      description: Mapping research insights to improvements (applied / derived)
    points:
      - title: Simpler linking, applied
        body: Simplified linking and onboarding, the biggest hurdle.
      - title: Preset modes and automation, applied
        body: For modes and automation that people couldn't picture using, offered favorite presets such as power ventilation, away mode and fine-dust automation.
      - title: More improvements derived
        body: Derived family sharing and member settings, alerts on by default, room- and home-level lighting groups, and a restructured home screen.
      - title: Community-facility alerts
        body: Residents also wanted alerts from community facilities such as the gym and pool.
impact:
  metrics:
    - value: 8% → 12%
      label: SmartThings adoption
    - value: 40–50% → 80–90%
      label: Adoption at complexes without their own app
  results:
    - Applied improvements such as preset-based modes and automation and simpler linking
    - SmartThings adoption rose from 8% to 12%, and from 40–50% to 80–90% at complexes without their own app
    - Established a repeatable research process that applies the same way to all three complexes
    - SmartThings smart apartments, first applied at Raemian Leaders One in 2020.11, passed 100,000 households by 2022.10 (112 complexes from 18 builders)
