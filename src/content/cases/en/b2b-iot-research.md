background:
  problem: To take B2C-focused SmartThings into B2B, we first had to find where the business opportunity was.
  context:
    - title: An undefined market
      body: We started without knowing who to sell to, or with what value.
    - title: A complex ecosystem
      body: Many stakeholders — developers, constructors, architects, IoT integrators, installers, managers, home-network suppliers, telecoms and residents.
    - title: Two countries
      body: Korea and the US differ in housing and decision structures, so the answers don't carry over.
approach:
  heading: Snowball research that grows from a hypothesis
  items:
    - title: Hypothesis and pilot
      body: Started from a hypothesis → first internal workshop (2018.11) → pilot IDIs (US 9, Korea 6).
    - title: Redefining the domains
      body: At the US workshop (2018.12), the Korea creative workshop (2019.01) and a second internal workshop, redefined the research domains as stakeholders, devices and context, and business logic.
    - title: A large main study
      body: Ran IDIs, shadowing and home visits at a scale of US 25+16 (San Francisco, Atlanta, Dallas, LA) and Korea 24+16.
    - title: Synthesis workshops
      body: Wrapped up conclusions and opportunity areas at synthesis workshops in the US (2019.02) and Korea (2019.03).
design:
  - label: US stakeholders
    heading: US — developers decide
    visual:
      id: b2b-stakeholders-us
      diagram: stakeholders-us
      description: US multifamily stakeholder structure (roles only)
    points:
      - title: Large multifamily developers
        body: In the US, large multifamily developers were the key target who decide on smart-home adoption.
      - title: Everyone else advises
        body: The owner (investor), design team, contractors and property management propose or advise to the developer.
      - title: Cost, energy and ROI
        body: Winning developers over hinged on proving cost and energy savings and ROI.
      - title: Opportunity — US MFU
        body: Multifamily units (MFU) became the US B2B opportunity area.
  - label: Korea stakeholders
    heading: Korea — constructors decide
    visual:
      id: b2b-stakeholders-kr
      diagram: stakeholders-kr
      description: Korean apartment stakeholder structure (roles only)
    points:
      - title: Constructors decide
        body: In Korea, the constructor was the key decision maker for adopting Home IoT.
      - title: Before sale and after move-in
        body: Before sale, developers, architects and home-network suppliers work with the constructor; after move-in, the residents' committee and management company take over operations.
      - title: Air quality matters most
        body: For Korean residents, air quality was the strongest value.
      - title: Opportunity — Korean smart apartments
        body: Smart apartments became the Korean B2B opportunity area and went on to become a business.
